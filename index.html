What this calculator does

A single-page, no-signup calculator to estimate:

• How your monthly expenses grow with inflation until retirement
• How big a retirement corpus you'll need
• What monthly saving (flat or step-up) gets you there
• How different savings options (SIP, PPF, EPF, FD, RD, NPS, Gold) compare
• A quick "safety net" check on emergency fund, life and health insurance

What the result labels mean

Conservative = assumes markets do worse than your expected return slider (−3 percentage points) → you'd need to save more per month to still hit the same retirement corpus

Expected = uses exactly your slider value

Optimistic = assumes markets do better than your expected return slider (+3 percentage points) → you'd need to save less per month

What the inputs mean

Inflation rateHow fast prices rise every year. If a ₹100 grocery bill costs ₹106 next year, that's 6% inflation. It quietly shrinks what your money can buy over time — future expenses look bigger than today's mainly because of this.
Return before retirementThe yearly growth rate you expect on money you invest while still working. Higher-growth options like equity have historically given more, but with real ups and downs year to year — which is why results are shown as a range, not one number.
Return after retirementThe yearly growth rate on your corpus once you stop earning and start withdrawing from it. Usually kept lower/safer than the pre-retirement return, since large swings are riskier once you depend on the money monthly. The corpus is designed to be fully spent down by your life expectancy age — it's not "live off the interest forever," it's a depleting balance that earns this return along the way.
Fixed vs. step-up savingA fixed SIP stays the same amount every month. A step-up SIP starts smaller but increases every year (e.g. alongside your salary) — usually more realistic for a growing career, and needs a smaller starting amount than a flat SIP for the same end goal.
Simple vs. custom inflationSimple applies one inflation rate to all expenses — good if you're unsure. Custom lets you split expenses into General / Education / Healthcare, each with its own inflation rate, since education and healthcare often rise faster than general costs.

How the numbers are calculated

Future expense = current expense × (1 + inflation) ^ years to retirement (or the weighted category version, in Custom inflation mode)

Corpus needed = present value of your inflation-adjusted monthly expenses over your retirement years, discounted at the "real" post-retirement return

Required saving (fixed) = flat monthly amount, growing at the pre-retirement return, that closes the gap between your current savings' future value and the required corpus

Required saving (step-up) = starting monthly amount that, increasing every year by your chosen step-up %, closes the same gap

Required salary today = current expenses + required monthly saving (Expected scenario) — the salary that covers both your current lifestyle and the saving needed to actually hit your retirement corpus

How "Monthly expense need" and "Corpus needed" relate

Monthly expense need at retirement = what your current monthly expenses will have grown into, after inflation, by the time you retire — in future rupees, not today's.

Corpus needed = the lump sum that, invested and earning your "return after retirement" rate, is exactly enough to fund that monthly expense (and its continued inflation) every month from retirement until your life expectancy — depleting to roughly zero right around that age, not before and not perpetually after.

Two caveats: (1) if you live past your chosen life expectancy, this corpus could run out — some people set it a bit higher than expected as a buffer. (2) it assumes the post-retirement return holds steady every year, which real markets don't guarantee.

How "Compare ways to save" and "Blended portfolio" connect to your corpus target

Their Total figure = (the monthly amount you set, grown at that option's/blend's rate) + (your current retirement savings, grown at that same rate over the same years) — compared directly against your corpus target, with a Covered (+surplus) or Shortfall status shown alongside.

About the savings options compared

Equity mutual fund (SIP)8–14% range. Market-linked, not guaranteed — a long-term historical reference for Indian equity. Any individual year (or stretch of years) can be flat or negative.
NPS (equity-heavy)8–12% range. Market-linked, mixes equity/debt/govt bonds. Partially locked in, with mandatory annuitization of part of the corpus at exit.
EPF / PF8.25% (FY 2025-26). Government-backed, rate set annually by EPFO. Contribution is a fixed % of salary shared with your employer, not discretionary.
Gold5–11% range. No interest or dividend — price appreciation only, can swing a lot over shorter periods.
PPF7.1% (current quarter). Government-backed, revised every quarter (unchanged since April 2020). Capped at ₹1.5 lakh/year, 15-year lock-in, fully tax-free returns.
Fixed Deposit (FD)6.5%. Bank-guaranteed (up to ₹5 lakh insured via DICGC). Roughly 6–7.5% for multi-year deposits as of 2026. Interest fully taxable.
Recurring Deposit (RD)6.5%. Same idea as FD for monthly deposits; similar bank-guaranteed rates and taxability.

All rates above are illustrative reference points as of 2026 and change over time — PPF/EPF are revised by the government, FD/RD vary by bank. Always check the current rate before actually investing.

About the safety-net check

A quick sanity check on basic protections — investing aggressively for retirement while missing these can backfire (e.g. having to break a long-term investment early because of an emergency).

1. Emergency fund

Target = months you choose (default 6) × current monthly expenses
Gap = Target − what you currently have saved

Example: ₹60,000/month expenses, 6-month target, ₹1,00,000 saved → Target ₹3,60,000 − Current ₹1,00,000 = Gap ₹2,60,000.

2. Term life insurance

Benchmark = 15 × annual expenses
Gap = Benchmark − your current life cover

A rough stand-in for a proper "Human Life Value" calculation (which normally uses income and dependents) — this tool only collects expenses, so this is an approximation. A real advisor's number will be more accurate, especially with dependents or a home loan.

3. Health insurance

Baseline = ₹10,00,000 (flat)
Gap = Baseline − your current health cover

The least personalized of the three — doesn't adjust for city, family size, or family floater plans, which typically push the real number higher.

Your basics

Inflation model

Not sure how to think about inflation? Use "Simple" — one rate for everything. If you'd rather account for education/healthcare rising faster than general expenses, switch to "Custom".

Remaining 75% of expenses treated as "general" at the general inflation rate above.

Saving strategy

A flat SIP stays the same amount every month. A step-up SIP starts smaller but increases every year — usually alongside your salary growth — which is more realistic for most careers.
