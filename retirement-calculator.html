What these terms mean

Inflation rate — how fast prices rise every year. If a ₹100 grocery bill today costs ₹106 next year, that's 6% inflation. It quietly shrinks what your money can buy over time, which is why future expenses look bigger than today's.

Return before retirement — the yearly growth rate you expect on money you invest while still working. Higher-growth options like equity have historically given more, but with ups and downs year to year — which is why we show a range, not one number.

Return after retirement — the yearly growth rate on your corpus once you stop earning and start withdrawing. Usually kept lower/safer, since you can't afford big swings once you depend on the money monthly.

Saving strategy

A flat SIP stays the same amount every month. A step-up SIP starts smaller but increases every year — usually alongside your salary growth — which is more realistic for most careers.

How this is calculated

Future expense = current expense × (1 + inflation) ^ years to retirement (or the weighted category version, in Custom inflation mode)

Corpus needed = present value of inflation-adjusted monthly expenses over retirement years, discounted at the "real" post-retirement return

Required saving (fixed) = flat monthly amount, growing at the pre-retirement return, that closes the gap between your current savings' future value and the required corpus

Required saving (step-up) = starting monthly amount that, increasing every year by your chosen step-up %, closes the same gap

Required salary today = current expenses ÷ (1 − savings rate)

About the savings options compared above

Equity mutual fund SIP — market-linked, no fixed rate; range shown is a long-term historical reference for Indian equity. Any individual year (or stretch of years) can be flat or negative.

PPF (Public Provident Fund) — government-backed, rate set by the Finance Ministry every quarter (currently 7.1% p.a., unchanged since April 2020). Capped at ₹1.5 lakh/year, 15-year lock-in, fully tax-free returns.

EPF/PF (Employees' Provident Fund) — government-backed, rate set annually by EPFO (8.25% p.a. for FY 2025-26). Contribution is a fixed % of salary shared with your employer, not a discretionary monthly amount.

Fixed Deposit (FD) — bank-guaranteed (up to ₹5 lakh insured per bank via DICGC), rate varies by bank/tenure; roughly 6–7.5% for multi-year deposits as of 2026. Interest is fully taxable at your slab rate.

Recurring Deposit (RD) — same idea as FD but for monthly deposits; similar bank-guaranteed rates and same taxability.

NPS (equity-heavy) — market-linked, mixes equity/debt/govt bonds; range shown is a long-term historical reference for an aggressive allocation. Partially locked in, with mandatory annuitization of part of the corpus at exit.

Gold — no interest/dividend, only price appreciation; range shown is a long-term historical reference and can swing a lot over shorter periods.

This is a planning tool, not financial advice. Rates for PPF, EPF, FD and RD are illustrative reference points as of 2026 and change over time (PPF/EPF revised by the government, FD/RD vary by bank) — always check the current rate before deciding. Equity, NPS and gold figures are long-term historical ranges, not promised or guaranteed returns, and actual results can be higher, lower, or negative in any period. Insurance and emergency-fund benchmarks are rough rules of thumb, not a personalized needs analysis. For decisions with real money on the line, consult a licensed financial advisor or insurance professional. Built for personal / educational use.
